# OAF Operating Model Redesign Workflow

## Purpose
Move from an evidenced operating-model diagnosis to a bounded redesign without jumping directly from symptoms to an org chart.

## Preconditions
Require:
- validated operating-model misfits;
- representative interface/decision evidence;
- explicit business outcomes and constraints.

Missing evidence may still permit framing options, but it may prevent selecting a final target model.

## Stages
1. **Confirm or reuse diagnosis** — do not restart generic analysis if a sufficiently evidenced diagnosis already exists. Use `operating-model-review`; add `global-local-model-review` only when global/local tension is material; add `organizational-interface-review` where interface failures dominate. If interface failures dominate but `organizational-interface-review` is unavailable in the active runtime, explicitly disclose that limitation, do not simulate the missing specialist, keep interface-specific causes provisional, and preserve a lower-disruption option until representative interface evidence exists.
2. **Resolve decision architecture** — use `decision-rights-review`; use `decision-bottleneck-analysis` where latency is a material symptom.
3. **Define design principles** — state which outcomes require enterprise consistency and which require local autonomy. Place authority with accountability and resources.
4. **Generate bounded options** — compare 2–3 genuinely different operating-model options, including a lower-disruption option where plausible. Do not optimize only for hierarchy.
5. **Test options** — authority/accountability/resources, interfaces, funding/capacity, architecture, decision latency, local responsiveness and transition risk.
6. **Separate framing from selection** — options may be framed under incomplete evidence; final selection requires enough evidence to distinguish material trade-offs.
7. **Select provisionally or leave unresolved** — if evidence supports a direction, label it provisional and define what could overturn it. Do not invent named owners when formal/actual ownership is unknown.
8. **Pilot for reversibility** — prefer a bounded capability/market pilot when the model can be tested before enterprise rollout.
9. **Design governance only after option choice** — use `governance-design` for the chosen decision model.
10. **Sequence change** — use `transformation-blueprint` only after the target direction is sufficiently decided.

## Decision rules
- Global consistency does not automatically imply central delivery.
- Local autonomy does not justify enterprise externalities without guardrails.
- Treat platform bypass/workarounds as evidence to diagnose, not automatic noncompliance.
- Shared accountability without funding/capacity authority is structurally weak.
- Do not choose a target model solely because it resembles a common best practice.
- Keep the lower-disruption option alive when evidence could still show that clearer rights/interfaces are sufficient.

## Output contract
- diagnosis recap;
- design principles;
- 2–3 bounded options;
- comparative trade-offs;
- provisional direction or unresolved choice;
- assumptions/evidence that could change the selection;
- critical design decisions;
- validation/pilot plan;
- next workflow/skill.

## Model guidance
Default: **standard**.
Validated on Luna for bounded redesign, option generation, trade-off analysis and pilot design.
A Sol-class strong model produced better selection discipline and alternative framing on the validated case but did not materially change the recommended direction.
Escalate to strong for:
- final high-impact target-state selection;
- materially conflicting executive evidence;
- large multi-country/global-local redesign;
- material transition economics;
- politically contested authority/funding choices.

## Stop conditions
Stop before design if evidence does not distinguish structural failure from poor execution of the current model.
Stop before final target-model selection if the evidence cannot distinguish the leading options.
