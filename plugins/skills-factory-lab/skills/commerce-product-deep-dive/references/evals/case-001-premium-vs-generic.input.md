# Commerce Case 001 — Premium vs Cheap Generic

## Scenario
A premium reusable pet-hair remover is sold in the US for the equivalent of 100–170 PLN per bundle. Similar generic tools are available in Poland for 20–35 PLN.

Target thesis:
- sell a premium branded version in Poland for 129 PLN;
- acquire customers mainly through Meta Ads;
- source from Asia with simple private-label packaging.

Known evidence:
- pet hair on car upholstery and sofas is a common visible problem;
- cheap generic removers already exist;
- premium US brands demonstrate the product effectively in short video;
- no evidence yet shows Polish customers will pay 3–5x generic prices;
- indicative COGS is 18 PLN per unit before freight/packaging;
- expected fulfilment/payment/packaging costs total 18 PLN per order;
- expected return/refund allowance is 8 PLN per order;
- target price is 129 PLN including VAT;
- for this synthetic case, use an explicit VAT rate of 23%; the listed COGS/fulfilment/payment/packaging amounts are net economic variable costs; the 8 PLN return/refund allowance is expected loss per order, not a second return-rate model;
- no validated CAC exists;
- supplier MOQ is 500 units;
- no unusual regulatory requirement is known beyond ordinary consumer-product obligations.

## User request
Use the `commerce-product-deep-dive` workflow.

Determine whether this is:
- TEST NOW;
- RESEARCH ONE BLOCKER;
- DEFER;
- KILL.

Do not treat premium branding as differentiation by itself.
Calculate CAC headroom.
Test why a customer would pay 129 PLN rather than 20–35 PLN.
Separate evidence, hypothesis and unknowns.
