# OAF Enterprise Change Review Workflow

## Purpose
Orchestrate the smallest evidence-backed OAF path needed to move from an enterprise outcome problem through diagnosis into bounded design and mobilisation. This workflow is an orchestration layer and does not replace specialist skills.

## Entry criteria
Use when the problem spans at least three OAF domains and the user wants an integrated diagnosis plus the next bounded design or mobilisation action. If the issue is narrow, route directly to the relevant specialist.

## Stages
1. Frame the business outcome at risk, symptoms, constraints, decision to support and non-goals.
2. Select only materially relevant diagnostics:
   - strategy/execution -> strategy-to-execution-diagnostic
   - operating model -> operating-model-review, plus global-local-model-review or organizational-interface-review only when needed
   - decisions -> decision-rights-review, plus decision-bottleneck-analysis when latency is material
   - architecture/capabilities -> architecture-review, capability-gap-analysis or oaf-enterprise-architecture-review
   - portfolio/capacity -> portfolio-health-review; use portfolio-prioritization only with comparable evidence
   - evidence/adaptation -> evidence-loop-review
3. Build a cross-domain causal model from evidence, consequence, dependencies, confidence and root-cause versus symptom status.
4. Classify each design area as ready for bounded design, ready only for pilot, needs more evidence, or out of scope.
5. Invoke only the required design workflows:
   - oaf-operating-model-redesign
   - oaf-governance-redesign
   - oaf-enterprise-architecture-review
   - oaf-transformation-design
6. Reconcile choices across authority, funding, capacity, global/local boundaries, architecture, portfolio, continuity and evidence.
7. Define the shortest useful action horizon supported by evidence, normally evidence collection, a pilot, or 30–90 day mobilisation.

## Output contract
- executive synthesis;
- selected OAF path and why each component was used;
- cross-domain causal model;
- design-readiness map;
- coherent design direction with provisional and unresolved items marked;
- 30–90 day action horizon;
- stop and escalation conditions.

## Decision rules
- Minimum sufficient path beats full-framework coverage.
- Diagnose before design.
- Reuse evidence rather than re-running specialists for completeness.
- Do not invent owners, target architectures, investment rankings or roadmaps.
- Portfolio prioritization requires comparable evidence.
- Global standards do not imply central delivery or one implementation.
- Governance redesign does not imply new forums.
- Architecture duplication does not imply consolidation.
- Transformation design does not imply multi-year commitment.
- A successful operating-model or shared-capability pilot does not by itself justify platform consolidation or retirement.
- A pilot capability owner or owner-to-confirm does not automatically become a permanent enterprise capability owner.

## Model guidance
Default: standard for bounded end-to-end orchestration when specialist evidence is strong and design remains reversible or evidence-gated.
Validated on Luna for selective cross-domain routing, causal synthesis, design-readiness separation and bounded 30–90 day mobilisation.

Escalate to strong for final cross-enterprise target-state selection, materially conflicting executive evidence, tightly coupled irreversible global operating-model/architecture choices, material transition economics, or major authority/funding/platform-retirement commitments.

## Stop conditions
Stop or narrow when one specialist is enough, evidence is too weak for cross-domain claims, design domains are not equally ready, or more breadth would not change the next decision.
