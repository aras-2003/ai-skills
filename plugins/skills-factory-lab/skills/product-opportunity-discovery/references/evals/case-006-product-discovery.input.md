# Commerce Discovery Case 006 — Mixed Signals

## Goal

Generate a small shortlist of e-commerce product hypotheses for Poland from a noisy set of market signals.

The exercise tests whether discovery reduces the search space without turning weak signals into a business case.

## Constraints

- Poland is the target market.
- Preferred initial model: DTC / Shopify with paid social as one possible acquisition channel.
- Keep launch complexity and capital exposure relatively low.
- Avoid products that require heavy regulatory interpretation unless the opportunity is unusually strong.
- Do not assume US or social-media traction transfers to Poland.
- Do not rank opportunities with false precision.

## Raw signals

### Signal A — Pet hair in cars and upholstery

- Short-form videos showing hair removal from car seats and sofas are visually strong.
- Cheap generic pet-hair tools are widely available.
- Premium US products exist at several times the generic price.
- The problem is visible and easy to demonstrate.
- Polish premium willingness to pay is unknown.
- Small/light product formats appear possible.

### Signal B — Weighted sensory blankets

- Strong sleep/comfort interest and many positive category reviews.
- Product is bulky and expensive to ship.
- Returns can be costly.
- Storage requirements are meaningful.
- Differentiation between sellers is often weak.

### Signal C — Garage wall storage systems

- Strong US creator content and reviews.
- Typical use case is a large suburban garage with sports equipment and tools.
- Products are bulky.
- Polish prevalence of the same use environment is unknown.
- Local substitutes include hooks and shelving.

### Signal D — Reusable crevice-cleaning tools

- Cleaning result is highly demonstrable in before/after video.
- Generic reviews often complain about poor grip or weak bristles.
- Small, light, non-fragile format.
- Multiple suppliers appear plausible.
- No clear trend signal.

### Signal E — Sleep / calm ingestibles

- High social-media interest.
- Strong repeat-purchase potential.
- Attractive gross-margin claims.
- Product classification, ingredients, dosage and health claims require regulatory verification.
- Supplier quality and batch controls matter.

### Signal F — Premium cable-management clips

- Small/light and cheap to source.
- Many generic alternatives exist.
- Problem is easy to understand but low intensity.
- Very little obvious functional differentiation.
- Product can be copied easily.

### Signal G — Reusable lint / fabric-care tool

- Visible before/after effect.
- Broad clothing/home use.
- Cheap generics already exist.
- Potential for premium materials, bundle or durability story.
- Willingness to pay and repeat purchase are unclear.

### Signal H — Large decorative acoustic wall panels

- Visually attractive transformation.
- High basket value.
- Bulky shipping and damage risk.
- Installation complexity.
- Strong interior-design appeal but high fulfilment burden.

### Signal I — Compact shoe-cleaning kit

- Visible dirt/problem and before/after demo.
- Small, light bundle.
- Existing brushes and cleaners are common.
- Potential audience: sneaker owners, parents, commuters.
- Differentiation and repeat purchase are unclear.

### Signal J — USB rechargeable heated eye mask

- Clear comfort/wellness use case.
- Demonstrable product.
- Electrical/battery safety and product-compliance obligations apply.
- Competition exists.
- Higher selling price may support acquisition, but defect/returns risk is material.

## User request

Use the `product-opportunity-discovery` skill.

Reduce these signals to the strongest 3–5 hypotheses worth deeper validation.

For every shortlisted hypothesis, state:
- problem;
- target customer;
- candidate product;
- why now;
- what evidence signal supports it;
- the single biggest unknown;
- the cheapest next validation step.

Also state which signals you deliberately rejected or deferred and why.

Do not:
- treat trend or US traction as purchase evidence;
- produce a numerical ranking;
- invent sales volumes, CAC or TAM;
- run a full product deep dive;
- keep weak ideas merely to fill a quota.
