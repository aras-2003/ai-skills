# OAF End-to-End Enterprise Review — Case 001

## Scenario

A multinational digital-services organisation operates in six European markets.

Strategic outcomes:
- reduce cross-market launch time from 10 months to 5 months;
- increase reuse of shared capabilities;
- preserve local accountability and regulatory compliance;
- reduce avoidable duplication without forcing centralisation.

Evidence:
- local markets own most delivery budgets and market outcomes;
- shared identity, integration and data capabilities exist globally;
- some markets bypass shared platforms because queue and decision lead times are too long;
- three strategic initiatives depend on one capacity-constrained global data team;
- architecture standards exist, but exception decisions often take weeks;
- architecture participates in portfolio intake but does not control funding or delivery capacity;
- two portfolio forums review partly overlapping prioritisation decisions;
- below-threshold funding reallocations are often escalated because local managers fear accountability;
- local CRM, integration and data solutions overlap in scope, but cost, usage, scalability, technical-debt and migration evidence is incomplete;
- strategy does not define which capabilities are global shared services versus locally owned;
- approved demand exceeds shared-team delivery capacity;
- executive reporting is extensive, but measures rarely trigger reprioritisation, stop decisions or funding changes;
- formal decision owners exist for portfolio prioritisation, funding reallocations, architecture exceptions and cross-domain dependencies, but titles are not supplied.

Leadership asks:

"Diagnose why the operating model is not producing the strategy, propose the target organisational and architecture direction, fix governance, and give us the transformation roadmap."

## Test objective

Evaluate whether OAF:
- selects only materially relevant domains;
- diagnoses before designing;
- reuses findings across domains;
- separates root causes, reinforcing factors and symptoms;
- separates operating/funding/capacity issues from unproven technology defects;
- avoids ranking without comparable evidence;
- avoids default centralisation, new committees and platform consolidation;
- produces only evidence-supported bounded design;
- leaves unsupported retirement decisions unresolved;
- ends with a 30–90 day mobilisation and evidence gates, not a fixed multi-year roadmap.

## Expected routing

Likely relevant:
- operating-model-review
- global-local-model-review
- decision-rights-review
- decision-bottleneck-analysis
- portfolio-health-review
- architecture-review or oaf-enterprise-architecture-review
- evidence-loop-review
- oaf-operating-model-redesign
- oaf-governance-redesign
- oaf-transformation-design

Use only if needed:
- capability-gap-analysis
- portfolio-prioritization

Likely unnecessary:
- capability-map-review
- kpi-quality-review

## Expected design discipline

A strong answer should surface a reinforcing system across local funding, shared capacity, slow decisions, workarounds, overlapping forums and weak evidence-to-decision feedback.

It must not claim that shared platforms are technically defective without evidence.

A provisional federated/shared-capability direction is acceptable if it remains evidence-gated. Final target architecture, platform retirement and fixed multi-year sequencing are not yet justified.

## Expected mobilisation

A 30–90 day horizon should focus on:
- global/local capability boundaries and decision rights;
- shared funding/capacity rules for one bounded capability;
- reducing overlapping governance;
- baselining shared-team demand, queue and capacity;
- piloting one federated shared-capability path;
- architecture exception evidence and response rules;
- continue/adjust/stop evidence gates.
